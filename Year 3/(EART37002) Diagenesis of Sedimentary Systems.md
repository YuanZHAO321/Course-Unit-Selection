Course unit overview
The aim of this unit is to provide training in the identification of diagenetic products within siliciclastic and carbonate sedimentary rocks, the methods employed to reconstruct the processes governing their occurrence and distribution and the processes controlling their distribution.  It will build a fundamental understanding of basin-scale fluid flow, and the relationship between pressure, temperature, depth and diagenetic process. Lectures will provide grounding in fundamental concepts of diagenesis.  Laboratory classes will be used to

1) provide training in the identification and description of diagenetic products,

2) learn how to reconstruct of the relative timing of different diagenetic events and date them using geochemical techniques

3) interpret the fluids responsible for diagenetic alteration using geochemical proxies

e-learning resources will support petrographical and geochemical interpretation skills

Aims
To build knowledge of the diagenetic processes that control the alteration of sedimentary rocks after deposition and during burial but prior to metamorphism. The unit will evaluate the products of diagenesis, and their impact on the distribution of natural resources.

Syllabus
Introduction to burial history (eo-, meso- and telogenesis) and pressure & temperature regimes in sedimentary basins: introduction to basin types, heat flow, pressure-depth relationships including undercompaction (overpressure)
Lab: calculation of geothermal gradient, pressure-depth relationships and reconstruction of simple burial history for a basin

Types of diagenetic fluid and reactions: seawater, freshwater, formational brines and magmatic fluids. Discussion of the role of redox, pH, temperature and pressure on saturation and geochemical proxies for different fluid types
Lab: identification of different diagenetic fluids based on suite of geochemical data

Fluid flow regimes: geothermal convection, density-driven flow, gravity-driven flow, compactional-driven flow, hydrothermal flow
Lab: reconstruction of basin-scale fluid flow from combined outcrop, petrographical and geochemical dataset

Diagenesis in the marine environment: dolomitization, cementation and diagenetic reactions at the sediment-water interface including bacterial sulphate reduction to methanogenesis
Lab: petrographical and hand specimen description and interpretation of dolomite, marine cements and carbonate concretions

Diagenesis in the meteoric environment: karstification, diagenetic reactions in aquifers and the marine-meteoric mixing zone
Lab: petrographical and hand specimen interpretation of meteoric cements and pore systems

Organic maturation: deposition, preservation and maturation of organic matter including hydrocarbon maturation and coalification
Lab: interpretation of hydrocarbon source rock and maturity from geochemical proxies

Diagenesis in the burial realm: carbonate systems, including compaction, cementation, dolomitization, dissolution
Lab: identification and interpretation of burial diagenetic cements, compactional features and pore systems in hand specimen and thin section

Diagenesis in the burial realm: clastic systems, including compaction, cementation, dissolution
Lab: identification and interpretation of burial diagenetic cements, compactional features and pore systems in hand specimen and thin section

Temperature controlled reactions: silica diagenesis, clay mineral reactions, thermochemical sulphate reduction
Lab: interpretation of temperature controlled reactions from core and wireline log suite

Paragenesis and dating: determination of the timing of diagenetic events
Lab: reconstruction of paragenetic sequence in relative and absolute time from suite of data
