## Course unit overview

The theory of plate tectonics has, since the 1960s, completely revolutionized the Earth Sciences.  It provides a basis for understanding a wide variety of Earth Science processes, from how sediments accumulate in foreland basins, to the rise of mountain ranges, to the cycling of chemical elements within the Earth.  It is important for the analysis of sedimentary basins hosting petroleum reserves.  It is also the background to many destructive geological events such as earthquakes and volcanic eruptions.

In this module, we will begin by learning about the methods used in tectonics.  In the oceans, these involve interpretation of seafloor spreading magnetic anomalies, information from earthquakes, bathymetry and gravity.  In the continents, the new methods of space geodesy have made significant contributions over the past 20-30 years, as well as earthquake seismology.

We will then look into processes occurring at mid-ocean ridges, transforms, volcanic islands and at convergent margins.  On the continents, we will investigate the extent that the crust is rigid or fluid-like, differences in earthquake behaviour, the response of the Earth to vertical loads and the role of surface processes (erosion and deposition).

## Aims

To gain more advanced understanding of:

1. The processes modifying and moving the solid earth beneath the oceans and on the continents, and the character of that movement
2. The techniques of oceanic and continental tectonic studies
3. Geodynamic analysis used to reveal the forces driving tectonic motions

## Syllabus

The course will be delivered with 1 hr lecture and 2 hr practical or workshop each week:

Week 1: The techniques of tectonic analysis

P: introduction to Geomapapp

Week 2: Creation of new oceanic crust at spreading centres 1

P: variations in crustal thickness from gravity data; volcanic geomorphology

Week 3: Creation of new oceanic crust at spreading centres 2

P: Earthquake focal mechanisms and size-frequency graphs

Week 4: Volcanic islands and seamounts

P: videos of active eruptions; morphometric measurements of seamounts

Week 5: Landslides and other mass movements

P: Using shear vane measurements from Amazon submarine fan to assess how channels change course.

Week 6: Subduction zones

P: sequence analysis of topographic profiles; gravity profiles and flexure; heatflow from bottom-simulating reflections (clathrates)

Week 7: Continental seismology - what earthquake distributions and properties tell us about strain

P: Mapping earthquakes with Geomapapp

Week 8: Forces driving plate motions

P: paper or computer-based exercise

Week 9: Flexure of lithosphere in the oceans and continents

P: paper or computer-based exercise

Week 10: Revision

## Teaching and learning methods

As this module tackles both historical and more recent research in tectonics, to do well (achieve >70%) you need to be willing to tackle research articles and work out effectively how you would build arguments based on them. Achieving a modest mark (~60%) should be possible using only the lecture material and your results from the practicals.
