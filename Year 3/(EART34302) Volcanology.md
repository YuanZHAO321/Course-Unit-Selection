## Course unit overview

The course starts by discussing what controls the distribution of volcanoes on Earth. We then examine the anatomy of volcanoes, and review processes of fractional crystallisation which lead to evolution of magma types (already covered in the igneous petrology course), followed by a quantitative examination of magma properties, and the relationship between viscosity, crystal volatile content, degassing and volcanic behaviour. This is followed by describing and quantifying different types of volcanic activity and related eruptive processes on Earth, and how to interpret eruptive products and deposits. We then examine volcano surveillance methods, hazard assessment and risk mitigation, concluding with the impact of volcanoes on climate and extinction events.

## Aims

The unit aims to allow students to develop an integrated view of volcanic processes from mantle to atmosphere, what controls the distribution and frequency of volcanic eruptions, and the impact of volcanoes on climate.

## Syllabus

- Week 1 L1: Introduction to Volcanology
- Week 2 L2: Volatile behaviour, P2: Estimate the volatile contents of primary magmas from diverse tectonic settings; Use solubility relationships for H2O and CO2 in basalts to predict volume at different pressures; Calculate viscosity as a function of H2O content.
- Week 3 L3: Magma evolution and magma chamber processes, P3: Thin sections of andesite examination: plagioclase zonation; Thin sections of a layered intrusion examination: eruptability; Exercise on Pressurisation of a magma chamber.
- Week 4 L4: Rheology and fluid mechanics, P4: computer practical, exercise 1: Etna magma viscosity vs H2O content and crystal volume fraction, Exercise 2: Vesuvius magma viscosity vs H2O content, crystal volume fraction and temperature, Exercise 3: Magma flow rates for Etna magmas, Exercise 4: Magma flow rates for Vesuvius magmas.
- Week 5 L5: Magma ascent and eruption, P5: Presentation in groups of 5 magma ascent papers.
- Week 6 L6: Eruption plumes and deposits, P6: Exercise class using equations to determine mass eruption rate, and deposit stratigraphy to determine eruption style.
- Week 7 L7: Volcano monitoring, P7: investigate volcano monitoring, what geophysical and geochemical, signals are produced and how are they measured and interpreted.
- Week 8 L8: hazard assessment and risk communication, P8: eruption crisis scenarios.
- Week 9 L9: Eruptions that shook the world: health and climate impacts, P9: SO2 emission quantification.
- Week 10 L10: Eruption case studies, P10: it will consist of group presentations on relevant eruptions of 10 minutes each with 5 minutes questions after each talk. This is a formative exercise and will not be assessed.

## Teaching and learning methods

Lectures to provide core material, with additional context from personal experience and ongoing real-time eruption activity as examples. Practicals to provide activation of the new knowledge, with focussed exercises to embed the new information, using computer cluster and group working. Supply of additional reading material from book chapters and publications to support each lecture.
